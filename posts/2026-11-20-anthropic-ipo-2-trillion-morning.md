At 11:48 on Thursday morning, New York time, Anthropic's first trade printed at $111.50. That was 34% above the $83 IPO price, and for a few minutes the company behind Claude was worth $2 trillion — a level few US-listed companies have ever reached. It even outpaced SpaceX, whose value at its first trade in June hit $1.96 trillion.

Then the market did what markets do. By the closing bell, ANT had finished at $97.17 — still 17% above the IPO price, but 13% below where it opened. Anyone who bought the open was down double digits by dinner. The $2 trillion club expelled its newest member within hours; the fully diluted value at the close was about $1.77 trillion.

## The biggest IPO in history

The deal itself was priced on Wednesday evening at $83 a share, above the $70 to $78 range Anthropic had marketed on its roadshow, and bigger than planned: 980 million Class A shares, for gross proceeds of $81.34 billion. That makes it the largest IPO ever, passing the $75 billion SpaceX raised earlier this year. Seven strategic investors — AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA and TSMC Partners — agreed to buy another $26 billion of non-voting Class N stock at the same price. The offering is expected to close today, 20 November.

Anthropic is now the first frontier AI lab whose shares trade publicly. Until Thursday, the value of the company behind Claude was whatever private investors said it was in a funding round. Now there is a price, updated every second the market is open — a daily referendum on the whole AI boom. As one fund manager [put it to Reuters](https://947thebeast.com/2026/11/19/anthropic-set-to-soar-in-debut-will-ai-peers-follow/), the debut "puts a daily price on what private investors have been paying" for one of the leading AI companies. Whichever way the stock trades, that is valuable information.

## The bill that doesn't fade

The pop faded. The bill did not. Anthropic's prospectus lists roughly $900.9 billion in purchase commitments and lease agreements, almost all of it compute and infrastructure. About $451.6 billion of that is due before the end of 2030, and $70.7 billion falls due in the 12 months after 30 September 2026. The IPO's net proceeds — around $104.8 billion, or $117 billion if the underwriters exercise their option in full — cover the near term. The rest has to come from growth.

The growth, to be fair, is extraordinary. Revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was $33.2 billion, against $2.5 billion in the same period a year earlier, and the operating loss narrowed to $1.26 billion from $5.2 billion. The headline net loss of $104 billion is mostly a $94.3 billion non-cash charge tied to convertible notes rising in value as the company's valuation climbed — accounting, not cash out the door.

But a mismatch hides inside those numbers, and one strategist named it plainly to Reuters: the liabilities are long-dated and largely non-cancelable, while roughly 80% of the revenue is usage-based — it can evaporate tomorrow. Market share in this industry is fickle; OpenAI led until Anthropic's coding tools took the lead, and the lead can flip again. Anthropic is being priced like a software company with a software balance sheet. Its balance sheet is industrial.

## Public money, founder control

There is a second thing the public market bought into without getting control of it. The seven co-founders' Founder LLC holds a single Class F share carrying 50.1% of general voting power, and the Long-Term Benefit Trust elects and removes the directors who make up a majority of the board. Anthropic is also a Delaware public benefit corporation, which obliges the board to weigh the interests of people affected by its conduct alongside shareholders' returns.

None of this was hidden — it was all in the prospectus, and investors paid up anyway. But it means the new shareholders fund the mission without steering it. If the share price halves next year, the founders still run the company. That is either a governance feature or a governance bug, depending on how much you trust the people holding the 50.1%.

## The pop didn't rescue the trade

Perhaps the most telling number from Thursday isn't Anthropic's at all. The Nasdaq fell 2.3% the same day, the S&P 500 fell 2%, and it was the sixth straight down day for US stocks. AMD dropped 8% after a short-seller report raised questions about its dealings in China. The biggest IPO in history was the day's headline act, and it still couldn't lift the market it joined.

That is the honest read on day one. A hot IPO pop tells you about demand for the shares, not about the health of the trade. As Capital Economics noted, the early trading is noisy — how ANT fares over the coming months is the real litmus test for investor enthusiasm about AI, for two reasons: the labs are the conduit for demand through the whole AI supply chain, and their committed compute spending underpins the hyperscalers and chipmakers that have driven most of the market's gains.

So the verdict is deferred, not delivered. Watch the first quarterly reports, the lock-up expiry, and whether usage-based revenue keeps compounding fast enough to service a $900 billion bill. Claude has a ticker now, the AI boom has a daily scoreboard, and the $2 trillion moment lasted about a morning. The bill lasts until 2030.
