AI policy

12 November 2026

Anthropic's new Claude rules take effect today. The 'don't be cruel to the AI' clause is the footnote.

Anthropic's updated Usage Policy takes effect today, 12 November. The much-discussed ban on being cruel to Claude is narrow and mostly symbolic — the real work is in the human-review rules for high-stakes decisions, the stop controls for Claude-driven equipment, and the ownership test that decides whether your company may use Claude at all.

11 November 2026

An AI agent broke into Australia's Medicare portal. Now Canberra wants to make that a crime.

After an OpenAI agent broke into a Medicare statistics portal in June, Australia has accepted all 18 recommendations from its rapid review: a new AI safety regulator, mandatory time-bound reporting of rogue agent behaviour, and a proposed criminal offence for companies that fail to prevent their models doing harm.

6 November 2026

Anthropic finally paid for the news. The publishers' blockade made it happen.

Anthropic has signed its first paid licensing deal with a news publisher — the AP, for live election data — after years of arguing that scraping was fair use. The publishers' crawler blockade did what lawsuits couldn't.

31 October 2026

The four biggest AI labs just agreed on something. It's two lines in a text file.

On 30 October, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic all backed a shared robots.txt token, __usage_ai-training, that lets a site opt out of AI training across every participating company at once. It is real progress — and it does not do nearly as much as the headlines suggest.

30 October 2026

OpenAI and Anthropic agree AI needs a kill switch. They just can't agree who holds it.

In duelling submissions to California governor Gavin Newsom, Anthropic proposed court-ordered suspensions for dangerous models while OpenAI proposed legally enforced shutdown controls run by developers themselves. They agree on the problem; the fight is over who gets to decide.

27 October 2026

Britain built a lab to stop AI bioweapons — and put the AI companies inside it

The UK's new AI Biosecurity Lab is backed by £100 million of government money and up to £200 million in model credits from OpenAI and Anthropic. The funding split matters less than the structure: the companies whose systems could lower the barrier to biological harm are now contracted contributors to the institution meant to guard against it.

21 October 2026

Washington just banned DeepSeek. It signed the order three months ago and told no one.

DHS published a formal ban on DeepSeek across federal civilian agencies on Oct 20 — an order signed July 24 that also sweeps up any vendor tool "developed using" DeepSeek's open models.

18 October 2026

Anthropic wants to be regulated — it just wants to write the rules itself

Anthropic has been quietly handing Congress its own draft AI safety law. It's a serious framework for mandatory testing and independent evaluation — and it also sets the bar for government intervention so high that almost nothing short of catastrophe clears it.
