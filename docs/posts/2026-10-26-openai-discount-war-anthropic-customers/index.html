OpenAI is buying Anthropic's customers — and the discount is the story

Replit, Rippling and DataCamp all moved the same week. Nobody argued about benchmarks.

There’s a particular kind of silence that tells you everything about a market. This week, three of Anthropic’s biggest customers explained why they moved their spending to OpenAI — and not one of them led with “the model got better.”

The reporting comes from The Information, which found OpenAI offering Anthropic’s large accounts discounts ranging from more than 30 percent to as much as 90 percent.

Start with Replit, the trophy. OpenAI reportedly offered more than 30 percent off a contract worth over $100 million a year, after Anthropic declined to extend Replit’s previous discount unless it committed to spending more. Replit chief executive Amjad Masad announced the switch on stage at TechCrunch Disrupt on 16 October, saying the company was no longer working with Anthropic and that the decision came down to “cost, partnership, and performance.” He added, bluntly: “I think ChatGPT is better now.”

Then he walked it back within a day. The softer version: OpenAI is now Replit’s “preferred partner” for Replit Agent, the company remains model agnostic, and seven Claude models are still listed in its catalogue.

Replit didn’t really leave Anthropic. It changed the default. OpenAI is now the default model for Replit Agent, and customers who want Claude have to go and pick it. Most users never change a default, so the default captures the bulk of the spending.

Rippling’s numbers are starker. Chief executive Parker Conrad said OpenAI’s share of the company’s coding workload rose from 16 percent in May to 52 percent, while Anthropic’s fell from 57 percent to 18 percent.

DataCamp is the smaller-scale version of the same playbook. Chief executive Jonathan Cornelissen said OpenAI’s pricing cut his costs by 80 to 90 percent. And then there’s the sharpest number in the whole report, which is also the smallest: a US bank with more than $7 billion in assets doubled its ChatGPT seats to 500 employees at no extra charge, paying under $7 per user against a usual rate of $20 or more. That isn’t a volume discount. That’s a customer being handed seats.

Now, the honest caveats, because the headline number deserves them. “Up to 90 percent” is not one published price cut. It’s a stack: contract discounts, switching to cheaper model tiers, bundled seats, workload optimisation. DataCamp’s 80 to 90 percent is one customer’s cost reduction, not a rate card. And all of this comes via a single outlet’s reporting — The Information is well-sourced, but OpenAI and Anthropic’s own silence in these reports means we’re reading one side’s deal book.

Even with those caveats, the shape of the story holds.

First: at the application layer, the two labs’ models are now close enough that price decides. Replit builds a coding product — the use case where model differences are supposed to be most visible and most expensive to get wrong. No benchmark appears anywhere in this story. If the switch had been about Claude losing on code, that would have been the headline. Instead, the reported trigger was a withdrawn discount. Wrapped in a harness, with retries and scaffolding around it, the models were substitutable enough that a 30 percent price difference outweighed whatever capability gap remained.

Second: OpenAI can afford this in a way Anthropic currently can’t, or at least won’t. The Decoder notes OpenAI has achieved genuine cost reductions through its Sol and Luna model lines — more efficient models and infrastructure — so not every discount is pure margin sacrifice. But buying market share is still buying market share, and it lands at an awkward moment for Anthropic: an investor memo cited in the reporting says its growth slowed from the second quarter to the third, and the company is reportedly weeks away from a listing. There is no worse moment to have your biggest logos quoted explaining how much cheaper the competition got.

Third: both companies are being squeezed from below at the same time. Chinese labs like DeepSeek and Kimi are delivering comparable performance for a fraction of the cost, and that pressure is what makes the discount war possible — and necessary. OpenAI isn’t cutting prices because it’s generous. It’s cutting prices because the floor keeps dropping, and the only thing worse than discounting your best customers is watching someone else do it.

So what does this mean if you’re not Replit? If you buy AI rather than build it, the practical lesson is blunt: your model provider is now a vendor, and vendors get negotiated with. Multi-model setups, defaults you can switch, and contracts that don’t punish you for leaving are worth more than any single model’s benchmark score — because the benchmark score is the thing that stopped mattering.

And if you build on top of these models, note what Replit actually did. It didn’t bet the company on OpenAI. It changed a default, kept the alternatives in the catalogue, and let the discount do the talking. That’s the template. The labs supplying the models are increasingly becoming the competition, and the smartest move in a price war is to make sure you’re never the one who can’t walk away.

The frontier model race was supposed to be decided by whoever built the smartest system. This week it was decided, at least for three large accounts, by whoever offered the better invoice. That might be the most important AI story of the month — precisely because it’s so boring.
