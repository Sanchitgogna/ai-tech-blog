AI tokens just got 90% cheaper. Don't believe the price tag.

Anthropic and OpenAI are racing to the bottom — and the bottom is subsidised.

Some weeks the AI industry announces smarter models. This week it announced cheaper ones — dramatically, suspiciously cheaper.

On October 7, Anthropic launched Claude Haiku 5.5 at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output: roughly 90% cheaper than Haiku 4.5 for prompts under 100K tokens. The interesting number isn’t the 90%. It’s the price itself — an exact match for OpenAI’s GPT-6 Luna. Anthropic didn’t undercut OpenAI. It photocopied them.

That’s not a price war. That’s a duel where both parties agreed to shoot blanks.

The good stuff is real

Let’s be fair first, because there’s a lot to like here. Haiku 5.5 brings adjustable effort levels (Low through Max) to the Haiku line for the first time, Anthropic cut Sonnet 5.5 cache reads by 50%, and Max and Team subscribers now get $100 to $500 a month in API credits. For anyone running AI agents — summarising documents, triaging tickets, doing the unglamorous bulk work — this is genuinely great news. One benchmark team reported their data analytics score jumped two letter grades at nine times lower cost.

Small models are no longer the embarrassing option. They’re the sensible one.

The cliff nobody advertises

There’s a catch, and it sits at 100K tokens. Cross that line and the price jumps fivefold. Not a gentle slope — a cliff. Developers planning long-context workloads have already flagged it as awkward: your cost planning is fine until one extra-long document lands and your bill does something alarming.

This is the part of the deal that’s worth reading twice. Under the threshold, you pay 2026’s bargain rate. Over it, you pay 2025’s ransom. Most of your prompts will never get near 100K tokens, so most of you can ignore this — but if your workload involves whole codebases or book-length documents, price the overage first, not the headline rate.

Why 90% off isn’t a cost curve

Now the real question: why would any company slash prices 90% in a week? Because these prices aren’t a cost curve. They’re customer acquisition.

The numbers floating around Anthropic tell the story: subscriptions reportedly bring in about 10% of revenue while consuming over 40% of inference compute. In other words, the cheap tier is a loss leader on a staggering scale — and Anthropic is about to host IPO investors on October 14. A 90%-off headline a week before that meeting is a market-share purchase, not a productivity miracle.

Subsidised prices end on the seller’s schedule, not yours. Budget 2027 at last quarter’s rates and you’ll never be caught out; budget at today’s rates and you’re building a house on a landlord’s introductory offer.

Meanwhile, OpenAI is decorating the free tier

OpenAI, for its part, is monetising from the other end. GPT-6 arrived with an “Intelligent UI” that renders responses as checklists, cards, and interactive layouts instead of plain text. The developer crowd’s reaction was prickly — one much-shared comment said the heavily formatted output feels condescending, like being talked down to.

Days earlier, OpenAI had announced a visual-ad format to be tested later in October in the US with selected advertisers, initially for Free and Go users during image generation. Ads, the company says, will be labelled and separate from what you generate.

Draw the lines between those two announcements and the picture gets clear: a prettier, more visual interface is also more ad inventory. The free tier’s job isn’t to be good — it’s to be colourful enough to sell.

What to actually do

This is the golden age of cheap AI, and you should enjoy it — just don’t trust it. Move your routine workloads onto the cheap tiers: the small models are genuinely good enough now for the boring stuff, which is most stuff. Keep an eye on that 100K-token cliff if you work with long documents. And when you see your free-tier interface getting prettier and more interactive, remember who’s paying for the prettiness. It’s not you, and that’s exactly the point.

The price tag says 90% off. The fine print says “for now.”
